# 03 Legal / compliance review: GymTrack on Google Play (2026-11-10)

This is not legal advice. Items marked **[pro]** need a German lawyer or tax adviser. The sources were seen on 2026-11-10. **[unverified]** means my own knowledge, not re-checked on the web today.

Repo facts: no INTERNET permission, no account, no analytics. `allowBackup=true` uses the template backup rules, which are empty (Android Auto Backup to the user's Google Drive is ON). There is no LICENSE file, privacy policy, Impressum, terms or OSS-notice screen. No bundled fonts (`FontFamily.SansSerif`). Dependencies: AndroidX core, lifecycle, activity, navigation, DataStore and Room 2.6.1, Compose BOM 2024.09.00, material3, material-icons-extended 1.6.5, Kotlin stdlib. All of these are Apache-2.0.

## A. Play Console declarations
- [ ] **Privacy policy URL** — Must · Missing · Play requires one even when the app collects no data. It must be a public, non-geofenced URL, not a PDF, labelled "privacy policy", and also linked inside the app ([Play User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)). Contents: the controller's name and contact; "data stays on the device"; what export/backup files contain (training history, notes, timestamps, bodyweight if added); that backups are unencrypted and the user is responsible for them; Android Auto Backup; deletion by uninstalling or clearing data; a promise to update the policy before adding crash reporting or sync. Host it on GitHub Pages or a simple personal site, in DE and EN. · S · #199 is related.
- [ ] **Data safety form** — Must · Missing · Declare "no data collected/shared" truthfully. Re-check this if crash reporting or sync is ever added. Whether Auto Backup counts as "collection" is **[unverified]**. Safest: decide on #199 and set explicit `dataExtractionRules`. · S
- [ ] **Health apps declaration** — Must · Missing · A required App-content form. Workout logging falls under "Activity and Fitness" ([Play help 13996367](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13996367)). · S
- [ ] **Target audience 18+ (or 16+), not "designed for children"** — Must · Missing · Choosing an age band under 13 triggers the Families policy and children's-data rules. Complete the IARC content rating questionnaire. · S
- [ ] **Trader status declaration** — Must · Missing · New accounts must declare immediately; existing accounts must declare by 2027-02-01. From 2027-03-01, traders' phone and address are shown publicly. Declaring non-trader tells EEA consumers that consumer-protection rights don't apply, and a false non-trader claim can lead to removal ([Play help 17594752](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752)). A free app with no monetisation is arguably non-trader. Paid apps, IAP, ads or a business purpose point to trader. A YouTube channel promoting the app could also count as business-related **[pro]**. If trader: consider a c/o address service or a cheap business address and phone, instead of the home address. · S
- [ ] **Developer verification / public account data** — Must · Partial (account status unknown) · Personal accounts show legal name, country and email. The full address is shown if the app is monetised ([Play help 10788890 / account info](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10788890)). · S

## B. German / EU law
- [ ] **Impressum (§5 DDG)** — Should (Must if trader or monetised) · Missing · Lawyers' view: a free app on a store is usually "geschäftsmäßig". The store's developer contact does NOT replace an in-app Impressum, which must be reachable within 2 clicks ([e-recht24](https://www.e-recht24.de/datenschutz/13272-app-veroeffentlichen-rechtliches.html), [IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/16-fragen-impressumspflicht-antworten-klarheit.html)). Recommendation: put the Impressum on the website and add an "About / Legal" screen linking to it, plus the privacy policy and licences. **[pro]** for purely private status. · S
- [ ] **GDPR, local-only** — Should · Partial · If the data never leaves the device to the developer, the developer arguably processes no personal data. The privacy policy still needs to say so. Fitness and body data (bodyweight, injuries in notes, future muscle-map pain or soreness flags) may count as **Art. 9 health data**: the CJEU reads "data concerning health" broadly (C-21/23, 2024-10-04 **[unverified]**). Consequence: any future server-side processing (crash logs containing notes, sync) needs explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)), a DPIA check, an Art. 28 processor contract, and EU hosting or a transfer basis. Rule: crash reporting must be opt-in and must exclude notes and workout content. · M (only when added)
- [ ] **Terms of use + disclaimer** — Should · Missing · For training suggestions (#308, #312): "general information, not medical advice; consult a doctor; train at your own risk". Under German law, liability for intent, gross negligence and injury to life, body or health **cannot be excluded** in standard terms (§309 Nr. 7 BGB **[unverified]**), so the disclaimer reduces risk but does not remove it. Keep suggestions conservative and based on evidence. · S
- [ ] **MDR boundary** — Must (as a design rule) · Have (intake non-goals) · Qualification depends on intended purpose. Wellness and fitness claims are outside the MDR ([MDCG 2019-11 rev.1, 2025-06-17](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en); [OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)). Never claim diagnosis, injury prevention or treatment, rehab protocols, or "detects overtraining or injury". Write a one-page "not a medical device" rationale in docs. · S
- [ ] **EAA / BFSG** — Nice · Partial · Covers listed services (e-commerce, banking …), not a free fitness logger. Microenterprises providing services (<10 staff, ≤€2M) are exempt ([overview](https://www.iubenda.com/en/help/179096/-eaa-compliance); vendor blogs, check the Directive Art. 4(5)). Do basic a11y anyway (content descriptions, contrast, font scaling). · M
- [ ] **Paid / IAP: tax** — Should (if monetised) · N/A now · Google is merchant of record for Germany ([Play help 7645364](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/7645364)). Income still needs a Gewerbeanmeldung, income tax, and a Kleinunternehmer (§19 UStG) check of the developer→Google supply. **[pro]** Steuerberater. · M
- [ ] **EU consumer law if paid** — Should (if monetised) · N/A · Google handles checkout and withdrawal-right waivers for digital content. Trader status then applies, and so do the digital content rules (§327 BGB: updates and conformity) **[unverified]**. · M

## C. IP
- [ ] **Open-source licence notices** — Must · Missing · Apache-2.0 §4 requires shipping a copy of the licence and any NOTICE files. Add the `com.google.android.gms:oss-licenses-plugin` or the AboutLibraries library, and an "Open-source licences" screen. If you later use the OFL fonts (Caveat, Patrick Hand), bundle the OFL.txt and don't sell the fonts on their own. · S
- [ ] **Name "GymTrack"** — Should · Risky · Existing apps: "GymTrack – Workout & Fitness" (CuriosCode, `com.cc_musculation`, on Play via AppBrain) and iOS "GymTrack: Workout Tracker", also offline-first ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gymtrack-workout-tracker/id6739433398)). The web search found no EUIPO/DPMA "GYMTRACK" mark, but **official registers were not searched** (do it on EUIPO eSearch, DPMAregister and TMview, classes 9/41/42). The name is descriptive, so registering it is hard and store-search discoverability is poor. Recommendation: use a distinctive brand ("GymTrack" only as a subtitle). **[pro]** for clearance. · S
- [ ] **Icon and artwork originality** — Should · Partial · The intake already requires provenance (`docs/intake/2026-11-10-feature-intake.md`). Keep the source files and dates for the icon, screenshots and muscle map. Don't use Google or Material marks in the listing. · S
- [ ] **Repo licence** — Nice · Missing · No LICENSE file. If the repo is public, that means "all rights reserved". Decide deliberately. · S

## D. Other
- [ ] **Export control** — Nice · Have · No custom crypto and no networking. Nothing to declare (Play has no export-compliance form, unlike Apple) **[unverified]**.
- [ ] **Unencrypted backup warning in the UI** — Should · Partial · Backup files contain potential health data. Show a warning when sharing a backup. · S · #199

## Top 10 by launch-blocking impact
1. Privacy policy at a public URL plus an in-app link (hard Play blocker).
2. Trader status declaration: decide trader vs non-trader before creating the account. If trader, get a business address and phone **[pro]**.
3. Data safety form plus the Auto Backup decision (#199).
4. Health apps declaration and target audience 18+, plus the content rating.
5. In-app "About / Legal" screen: Impressum link, privacy policy, OSS licences.
6. OSS licence notices for Apache-2.0 dependencies.
7. Name clearance: search EUIPO/DPMA, consider a distinctive brand.
8. Disclaimer and terms before shipping suggestions (#308/#312). MDR-safe wording.
9. Rule: future crash reporting or sync must be opt-in, exclude content, and go through an Art. 9 review.
10. Before any monetisation: tax and Gewerbe check with a Steuerberater.
